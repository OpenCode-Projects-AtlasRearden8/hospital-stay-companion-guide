# The Teach-Back Card

> **One page, printable, the polite superpower.** Teach-back means
> playing back what you heard *in your own words* and letting the
> professional correct you. It catches miscommunication at the
> cheapest possible moment — while the teacher is still standing
> there. Full how-to lives in [chapter 4](../04-teach-back.md).

## The universal opener

- "Just to make sure I understand — I want to play back what you
  just said: you're saying… [your words]. Is that right?"

## At admission: the four playbacks

1. **Today's plan:** "Quick playback of this morning's plan so I've
   got it: [*your words*]. Right?"
2. **Who's in charge:** "Who runs the overall plan this week, and
   who do we ask when that person isn't here?"
3. **What changes and what doesn't:** "So the routine tonight is
   unchanged until the team says otherwise — correct?"
4. **The family loop:** "Both of us are on the
   may-discuss-this-with list, and you'd call [*name*]'s phone
   first — is that in the record?"

## At discharge: the seven playbacks

1. **Meds:** "This list replaces what she takes at home; the
   antibiotic is ten days *all of them*; what changed is [*…*]."
   (Full decode: [chapter 5](../05-discharge-checklist.md).)
2. **Care at home:** "We can do the dressing changes alone — but
   the first time we'd like a home health nurse to show us. Normal
   to ask?"
3. **Warning signs:** "The *call today, don't wait* signs are:
   [*read the sheet back verbatim*]. Right?"
4. **Restrictions:** "No driving two weeks; nothing over ten pounds
   (a gallon of milk) for four; stairs fine but slow."
5. **Follow-ups:** "Dr. [*name*] in about ten days, and I book by
   calling [*number*] — got it written and in my phone."
6. **Call numbers:** "Weekday line [*number*], nights-and-weekends
   [*number*], and we don't have to be *sure* anything's wrong to
   call."
7. **The one-thing check:** "In one sentence — what's the one
   thing you most want us not to miss this week?"

## The three assistant rules

- The **question** is ours; the **answer** is theirs. Playback in
  our words, corrections from the care team.
- **"Not sure" is a call-worthy sentence.** On-call line, 11 p.m.:
  "I'm not sure which side of the line this is on" is complete.
- **Never translate playback into decisions.** Education only —
  never medical advice; care decisions stay with the care team,
  always.
