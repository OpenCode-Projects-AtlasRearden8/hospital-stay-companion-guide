# Disclaimer

## The whole promise, in one place, in plain words

**This is education only. This is never medical advice.**

The Hospital Stay Companion Guide is a practical, plain-English manual
about the *logistics* around a hospital stay: packing, forms,
bedside-helping, question-asking, checklist-decoding, homecoming
setup, and bill review. It is written by a general author team for
general readers. It is not a medical document, and it never forms a
caregiving relationship of any kind.

## What this guide is not

- **It is not medical advice.** Nothing here diagnoses, treats,
  recommends, or rules on any condition, symptom, medication, or
  treatment choice. Every care question belongs to your care team —
  your nurses, your doctors, your pharmacist — who know the patient
  and the situation and are trained for exactly those questions.
- **It is not legal, financial, or insurance advice.** Forms,
  billing, and insurance are explained as *paperwork to read*, not
  decisions to make. For anything specific — an advance directive, a
  billing dispute, an insurance appeal's strategy — the right
  professional is the one who can see your real documents.
- **It is not a substitute for the care team's instructions.** Where
  this guide and your discharge paperwork differ, the paperwork and
  the people behind it win, in that order. Ask the question we
  helped you write; that's translation, not replacement.

## What this guide is

- A way to make the forms, folders, and phone calls feel less
  mysterious, so you can ask better questions and write better notes.
- A set of *organizational* tools: packing lists, teach-back
  sentences, checklist decoding, one master-note habit.
- A bridge between companions in the same family of guides — the
  *medical-bills-comfort-guide* (life after the envelopes start
  arriving) and the *free-health-admin-kit* (everyday health
  paperwork) — where the heavier administrative lifting lives.

## Emergencies (short and serious)

If anything is evolving fast and looks like an emergency — this
manual does not define symptoms; trust your own alarm — **use the
emergency services your region provides: 911 in the United States**,
the numbers on your discharge folder's call page, or the nearest
emergency department. A guide about paperwork never outranks a
feeling in the room. We'll say it once more, in the only capital
letters this guide owns: **in an emergency, call for help; do not
consult a manual about packing.**

## The ordinary fine print

- Examples use invented people and round numbers ("Dr. Ruiz," "a
  gallon of milk," "ten days"). Any resemblance to actual persons is
  coincidental, and no real patient's information appears anywhere in
  this project — there are no personal accounts, no case stories
  borrowed from real charts, nothing private.
- Hospital-specific details (which form asks what, what a floor
  supplies, what a discharge folder includes) genuinely vary. This
  guide describes common patterns; your hospital's version of paper
  is the one you play their local version against. When in doubt,
  ask, and the answerers — every time — are the ones on the
  premises.
- Nothing in this project is connected to any medical institution,
  insurer, or company. No product is recommended; no service is
  endorsed; nothing here sells you anything.
